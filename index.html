People (directors / shareholders)

Assumptions: NIC is on the simple annual basis; the Employment Allowance is not available to companies whose only employee is a single director. Employer pension contributions are assumed within the annual allowance and valued at the amount paid in — drawdown is taxed later (normally 25% tax-free), which is not modelled here. Planning aid, not advice.

Use of home — director charges the company rent

Under a licence, the director can charge the company a market rent for use of part of the home. The rent is deductible for the company (corporation-tax relief) and taxed on the director as property income — but only on the profit after an apportioned share of household running costs. Matching the rent to those allowable costs extracts cash at little or no personal tax.

Notes: set a commercial rent and put a licence agreement in place. Apportion costs on a just-and-reasonable basis (e.g. business rooms ÷ total rooms, then by time used). Keep the business use non-exclusive so Private Residence Relief is not lost on a future sale. A rental loss generally can't be used to extract more than the costs. Planning aid, not advice.

Employing your children (13+) — shift income to a family member's allowances

A child aged 13 or over can be employed for genuine work. A commercial wage is deductible for the company, and the child has their own personal allowance — so a wage up to the allowance is tax-free in their hands, moving income off the parents' higher rates. It only works if it's done properly (see the conditions below).

How to justify it (get this right or HMRC can disallow the deduction)

  • Real work, commercial rate. The child must actually do the work (e.g. social media, filing, packing, cleaning) and be paid the going rate for it — no more. The deduction must be wholly and exclusively for the trade; HMRC can restrict an excessive family wage (the classic authority is Copeman v William Flood & Sons Ltd [1941], where wages to a director's son and daughter were challenged as excessive).
  • Pay it for real. The money must actually be paid to the child, into the child's own bank account — not retained by the parents.
  • Keep records. Timesheets / a simple log of duties and hours, and a basic contract. Operate PAYE if required (even if no tax is due, report if over the reporting threshold).
  • Age & hours rules. Minimum age 13 for light work; under-16s need to follow the local council's child-employment byelaws (often a permit) and strict limits on hours/term-time. National Minimum Wage doesn't apply below school-leaving age, but the rate must still be commercially justifiable.
  • NIC. Under-21s have a nil employer-NIC band up to the upper secondary threshold, so employer NIC is usually nil on a modest wage.
Planning aid, not advice — confirm the position and local byelaws before acting.
Relevant Life Plan — tax-deductible life cover through the company

A Relevant Life Plan is a single-life death-in-service policy the company takes out on a director/employee and writes into trust. The premiums are generally corporation-tax deductible, with no benefit-in-kind and no NIC, and the payout is normally free of income tax and outside the estate for IHT — far cheaper than paying for the same cover from taxed income.

Conditions to qualify

  • Single life, death-in-service (and usually terminal illness); term typically ends by about age 75.
  • Written into a discretionary trust for the family from outset; no surrender/investment value.
  • For an employee or director of the company (not available to a sole trader/equity partner on themselves).
  • Premiums must meet the wholly & exclusively test — generally fine for a normal level of cover linked to remuneration.
Planning aid, not advice — set up via a suitable provider and trust.

How the comparison works

  • Permanent cost = dividend tax + income tax on the loan benefit-in-kind + employer Class 1A NIC. This money never comes back.
  • S455 = 35.75% (2026/27) of the loan still outstanding 9 months and 1 day after the company year end. It is refunded under s458 once the loan is repaid, so it is a cash-flow / timing cost, not a permanent one — shown separately.
  • Benefit in kind applies whenever the loan exceeds £10,000 at any point in the year. It is taxed on the average balance at the official rate of interest as employment income (so it stacks on salary), and the company pays Class 1A NIC on it.
  • Tax on tax: if a dividend is declared to fund a tax bill, that dividend is itself taxed — model it by entering the gross dividend needed. For an additional-rate payer there is no rate saving from spreading dividends (all at 39.35%), so declaring dividends purely to clear a refundable S455 charge can be the most expensive route.
  • Bands & allowance: the model applies the personal allowance taper (£1 lost per £2 over £100k) using adjusted net income, and taxes dividends across the ordinary / upper / additional bands on top of other income.
